What Is Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana?

Understand the basics of crop insurance in India, why it matters to farmers, which risks may be covered, and how applications and claims generally work.

A practical guide to agricultural risk protection
📘 Educational guide 🌾 Agriculture and insurance Reading level: Beginner

Farming depends on many factors that cannot always be controlled. Rainfall may arrive too late, a flood can damage standing crops, or pests and diseases can reduce a harvest. Even after months of work, farmers may earn less than expected because of events beyond their control. Crop insurance is one way of managing some of these financial risks.

In India, the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana, commonly called PMFBY, is a government-supported crop insurance scheme introduced to help eligible farmers manage specified crop losses. This beginner's guide explains its purpose, basic terminology, the kinds of risks that may be insured, and the steps farmers should understand before applying.

The simple definition

Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) is a crop insurance scheme designed to provide financial protection for eligible farmers when insured crops suffer qualifying losses from specified, non-preventable risks, subject to scheme rules and seasonal notifications.

1. What Is the Main Purpose of PMFBY?

PMFBY was introduced in 2016 to improve access to crop insurance and help reduce the financial impact of agricultural production risks. A crop can fail or produce a lower yield for reasons that are difficult for an individual farmer to prevent. When income falls, the farmer may still need to repay loans, purchase seeds, pay workers, and prepare the land for the next season.

Insurance can help eligible farmers manage part of this uncertainty. Under an applicable policy, a claim may be payable when the insured crop experiences a covered loss and all relevant conditions are met. It is not a promise that every loss will be reimbursed, nor is it a guaranteed payment to every person who cultivates land.

2. Why Is Crop Insurance Important?

Agriculture is exposed to both natural and economic uncertainty. Farmers make investments before knowing the final harvest result. Seed, fertilizer, irrigation, machinery, transport, and labour may require spending weeks or months before income is received.

Consider a farmer who grows a notified crop and spends money preparing the field. Heavy rainfall then damages the crop. The farmer may harvest less than expected, but many production expenses have already been incurred. If the loss meets the applicable insurance conditions, crop insurance may help reduce the resulting financial burden.

Insurance does not eliminate every agricultural risk. Market-price changes, poor farm management, excluded events, and other uncovered circumstances may still affect income. Farmers should therefore consider insurance alongside weather information, soil management, suitable crop selection, water conservation, and careful financial planning.

3. What Types of Crop Losses May Be Covered?

Coverage depends on the scheme's applicable provisions, the notified crop and area, the season, and the relevant insurance arrangements. PMFBY guidance describes several categories of risk. Their availability and claim conditions should always be checked for the particular crop and location.

Risk category What it generally means
Yield losses A qualifying reduction in crop yield associated with specified covered risks, assessed under the applicable scheme method.
Prevented sowing Certain situations in which adverse conditions prevent sowing in a notified area, subject to scheme requirements.
Localized calamities Specified localized events, such as certain hailstorm, landslide, or inundation losses, when applicable conditions are satisfied.
Post-harvest losses Certain notified losses affecting harvested crops left in the field for drying, within applicable time and event limits.

Depending on the covered event and policy conditions, risks may include drought, dry spells, floods, inundation, storms, hailstorms, cyclones, pests, or plant diseases. This list is educational rather than a guarantee that every event is covered in every area.

Important: A weather event or crop problem does not automatically qualify for compensation. The notified crop, location, season, type of loss, reporting procedure, assessment method, and exclusions can all matter. Always read the current notification and applicable policy conditions.

4. Who Can Participate in the Scheme?

Eligibility should be checked against the rules applicable to the relevant state, crop, and season. PMFBY has included eligible farmers cultivating notified crops in notified areas, subject to the scheme's terms. The exact procedure and available crops may differ between locations and seasons.

Farmers should not assume that a landholding, bank account, or previous participation alone guarantees eligibility. Cultivation details, the crop selected for insurance, required records, deadlines, and local notifications may affect an application.

Questions to check before applying

  • Is the crop notified for insurance in the relevant area and season?
  • Does the applicant meet the applicable farmer and cultivation requirements?
  • Is enrolment open, and what is the notified deadline?
  • Which documents and land or cultivation records are required?
  • What premium, sum insured, and coverage conditions apply?

5. What Documents May Be Needed?

The required documents can depend on the enrolment channel, farmer category, state instructions, and current season. Applicants should check the official requirements rather than rely on an old checklist. Commonly requested information may include:

  • Identity and contact details.
  • Bank account details for eligible claim payments.
  • Land records or other accepted evidence of cultivation, where required.
  • Details of the crop, area cultivated, and season.
  • Application or policy acknowledgement, if issued.

The information submitted should be accurate and consistent across the application and supporting records. Keep copies of the submitted documents, receipts, policy details, and any messages received about the application. Do not share banking passwords, PINs, or one-time passwords with anyone claiming to help with enrolment.

6. How Does the Application Process Work?

The process is not identical everywhere, but the following stages provide a useful general overview. The actual steps, service channels, and deadlines must be confirmed for the relevant crop season.

1

Check the seasonal notification

Confirm the notified crop, location, enrolment window, insurer or implementing arrangements, and applicable terms.

2

Prepare the required information

Gather the documents and cultivation details requested for the relevant application channel.

3

Submit the application

Use an approved channel available for the scheme and retain the acknowledgement or reference number.

4

Review the policy information

Check the insured crop, area, season, premium details, and other policy information for errors.

5

Follow instructions if a loss occurs

Report a qualifying loss through the required channel and within the applicable deadline. Keep records of the report.

7. How Do Crop Insurance Claims Work?

A claim is a request for insurance benefits after a covered loss. It is different from an application to purchase insurance. A claim must be considered under the policy terms and applicable scheme procedures before a payment can be approved.

Depending on the category of loss, assessment may involve notified area yield data, crop-cutting experiments, field-level information, weather records, or other prescribed evidence. Some loss categories may require individual reporting, while others follow an area-based assessment approach.

Farmers should pay close attention to deadlines for reporting localized or post-harvest damage. If a loss occurs, record the date, location, crop, and nature of damage. Follow the current official reporting instructions and preserve any acknowledgement. Photographs can be useful records, but they do not replace required official reporting or assessment procedures.

Why might a claim need further review?

  • The application or insured crop details need verification.
  • Required loss information or documents are incomplete.
  • The loss must be assessed under the applicable scheme method.
  • Bank or policy information needs correction or confirmation.
  • The claim is being processed by the relevant authorities or insurer.

These are general examples, not a statement about any individual claim. The responsible authority or insurer is the appropriate source for a specific claim decision or payment timeline.

8. Understanding Crop Insurance Terms

Term Plain-language explanation
Premium The amount paid by the insured farmer for insurance, according to applicable rules.
Sum insured The insurance amount determined using the applicable scheme and crop details.
Claim A request for benefits following a potentially covered loss.
Notified crop A crop included in the relevant official notification for a specified area and season.
Yield assessment A prescribed method for estimating crop production and assessing qualifying losses.
Policy The record of insurance coverage and the terms that apply to it.

9. Common Misunderstandings About PMFBY

Every farmer automatically receives a payment

Insurance benefits are not automatic. Enrolment, eligibility, coverage, assessment, and claim conditions all matter. A payment should not be assumed simply because a crop has been damaged.

Every type of agricultural loss is covered

Coverage applies to specified risks and conditions. Losses caused by excluded events or circumstances may not qualify. The applicable policy and notification explain the relevant limits.

A website displaying scheme information is always official

Government-scheme information may appear on independent educational websites, news pages, and other online resources. A website's appearance alone does not establish official status. Check the domain and confirm important information through government sources.

A submitted application means insurance is confirmed

Submission and confirmation are not necessarily the same thing. Farmers should retain the acknowledgement and check the policy or application record for confirmation and any corrections needed.

10. How to Find Reliable PMFBY Information

Scheme rules and operational details can change by season and location. When looking for information about eligibility, premiums, crop notifications, deadlines, or claims, prefer official government resources and current notifications over undated social media posts.

If information conflicts across websites, rely on the latest applicable government notification or clarification from the responsible authority.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does PMFBY stand for?

PMFBY stands for Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana, India's government-supported crop insurance scheme introduced in 2016.

What is the main objective of PMFBY?

Its main objective is to provide financial support for eligible crop losses caused by specified covered risks and help farmers manage agricultural production uncertainty.

Does PMFBY cover every crop?

No. Coverage depends on the crops and areas notified for the relevant season and the scheme's applicable conditions.

Can a farmer claim compensation for any crop damage?

Not necessarily. The cause of loss, coverage category, reporting requirements, assessment, and policy terms determine whether a claim may be admissible.

Where can farmers check official information?

Farmers can consult the official PMFBY portal, relevant state agriculture department notifications, and the responsible insurer or authorized service channel for their area and season.

Does applying guarantee a claim payment?

No. Enrolment does not guarantee payment. A claim must satisfy the applicable coverage and assessment requirements.
